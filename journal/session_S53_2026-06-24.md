# Session S53 — 2026-06-24
**Topic:** aura-sweet

## The Problem
Aura Sweet had a product, real early sales, and a poster that was already converting — but no name identity. The four flavors were still described by their base ingredients: Thai Tea, Fish Sauce Caramel, Coconut, Mango Sticky Rice. Commodity names for what should be a proprietary brand.

## Questions We Were Trying to Solve
- Should the flavors have unique branded names or stick to descriptive ingredient names?
- What naming theme ties all four together without feeling forced?
- Thai-language names — smart or too hard to say?
- What should the poster include beyond the flavor names?
- How would Jonah Berger, Seth Godin, and Gary Vee actually campaign for a 500-follower local Thai restaurant launching an ice cream concept?
- Should the fish sauce reveal be teased early or held back?
- Does Aura Sweet need a second mystery poster or does one poster do both jobs?

## What We Tried That Didn't Work
- **Precious metals theme** (Gold, Amber, Pearl, Silk) — too abstract, didn't feel like food
- **Thai-language names** (Cha Yen, Khao Niao, Maprao) — authentic but create friction at point of sale; people can't say them to a friend easily
- **Four rounds of naming sets** before landing on the right theme — each round narrowed the direction until Option C (food/story forward, slightly mysterious) emerged as the clear winner
- **Campaign v1** — 8-post, 4-phase content calendar. Wrong on two counts: too big for a local launch, and revealed the fish sauce ingredient in the TEASE phase. Chris caught it immediately: "If you show fish sauce in the first post you've already revealed the secret." Correct. Scrapped.
- **The mystery poster** — dark background, Chef's Secret only, "What's in it?" headline. Good concept but Chris confirmed one poster is enough. The menu poster already carries both jobs.

## What We Built
Four flavor names that feel proprietary, easy to say, and connected to a theme. The Brew, Chef's Secret, Island Cream, Sweet Grain. All land in the same register — food-forward, slightly evocative, nothing you'd see on a generic Thai ice cream menu anywhere.

The poster copy came together around the names: "Small batch · Made in-house · Thai-inspired" as the header tagline, short italic descriptions under each name, "Original recipes. Nobody else's." as the brand claim, "Try all four. Tag us when you do." as the CTA, with @aurathaiLB anchoring the footer. A complete piece that works without any campaign context.

The campaign that emerged when the masters were applied correctly was three moves — not eight posts, not a content calendar, not a brand awareness phase. Three moves calibrated to a 500-follower local restaurant with a 10K-follower collaborator and 140 scoops already sold.

Move 1 is the proof post — 140 scoops with no campaign is stronger social proof than any manufactured launch hook. Berger's logic: remove the risk for the next person by showing it's already happening. Move 2 is the BKBA collab — Godin's logic, reach the right 10,000, not the wrong 100,000. Move 3 is the Chef's Secret video — GaryVee's logic, document the real reaction, don't create a produced moment. The fish sauce reveal is held until this video. It fires once. It earns its fire.

## Key Decisions
- **Flavor names locked:** The Brew / Chef's Secret / Island Cream / Sweet Grain
- **Theme:** Food/story forward, slightly mysterious — Option C direction confirmed
- **No Thai-language names** — friction at the point of sale kills word of mouth
- **No prices on the poster** — masters never put prices on brand posters; it dates and cheapens
- **One poster only** — the menu poster does both the sell job and the campaign anchor job
- **Chef's Secret reveal is a one-time asset** — never teased early, saved entirely for the reveal video
- **Campaign scope matched to business size** — 3 moves, not a content calendar
- **BKBA is the primary reach channel** — 10K hyper-local Long Beach followers beats any ad spend

## What's Alive Now

- Menu poster mockup: HTML complete, copy locked, ready for Canva
- Campaign v2: 3-move launch playbook — all three moves ready to execute
- Poster-to-campaign connection: every move references the poster (blurred in tease, hero at launch, backdrop for the video, photo prop for UGC)
