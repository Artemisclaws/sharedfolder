# ROAMWITHCHRIS — STANDING CAPTURE KIT
**Shoot this on EVERY trip, before any story exists.** The Bible tells you what a specific story needs; this list is what every story needs. Chris already over-shoots by instinct — this aims the instinct.

## The Always List (every trip, no exceptions)
1. **Faces reacting** — not posing. Catch the half-second after something happens: surprise, exhaustion, laughing at a failure. Reaction shots are the emotional cut points in every edit.
2. **Hands doing things** — packing, cooking, tying, pointing at a map, holding Auggie. Close-ups of hands are the universal B-roll connector.
3. **Feet walking** — on every surface type. Transition currency.
4. **Thresholds** — trailhead signs, doorways, airport gates, "entering X" moments. Every act break needs one.
5. **Wide establishing** — where are we? One slow, steady wide per location. Hold 5+ seconds; you'll speed-ramp it later.
6. **The boring connective tissue** — loading the car, waiting, setting up camp, the drive. This is what "and then we got there" cuts are made of. Nobody shoots enough of it.
7. **One honest low moment** — someone tired, frustrated, cold, done. The Turn and the stakes ladder live here. Without a documented low, every story flattens into a highlight reel.
8. **Cast trait shots** — Kate planning/reading a map, Golfii pushing through something, Pumpkin being the veteran, Auggie watching/eating/reacting. One per cast member per trip keeps the parasocial traits stocked.
9. **Cover candidates** — 3–5 deliberate frames per trip that could stop a thumb at grid size: motion, tension, or an expressive face, subject centered (grid crops to 4:5/1:1, text zone is center-frame).
10. **Audio wilds** — 30 seconds of clean ambient per location (wind, water, street, silence). The wind-clip trick from Havasupai only works because the audio existed.

## The 48-Hour Rule
Within 48 hours of getting home (or on the drive), **voice-memo the story to yourself** — just tell it like you'd tell a friend. The natural beats, the lesson, and the Turn almost always surface in the raw telling. This memo is the seed of the Bible; without it, beats get reconstructed from memory weeks later and go flat. (Chris rule: never rely on memory — record it.)

## Format Discipline
- Shoot vertical-first or center-punch-safe (critical action in the middle third) so one master works for Reels/Shorts/TikTok.
- Hold every shot 3 seconds longer than feels natural. Cuts under 1 second need clean heads and tails.
- When in doubt: shoot the reaction, not the thing. The waterfall is stock footage; Golfii's face at the waterfall is the story.
